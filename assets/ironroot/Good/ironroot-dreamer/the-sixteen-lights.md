# The Sixteen Lights

> Sky law: the lights are named fires, ancestors, wanderers, roads, and wounds. They carry stories and directions — never identities, never fates, never a sign-system. Engine names stay as glosses. ## The Ledger's Eye (Sirius)

**Tier:** GuideStar | **Spectral Class:** AskiyGold

High over the Cathedral at first dark, where the bell-rope attaches to stone. A light that watches the watchers, unchanging through every era-turn.

It held its place through centuries of Root-Pruner nights: ledger-lamp below, its own fire eternal above. When the Knife retired, it flared once—not dimming like mortal things, but burning sharper. When Morrow's name was sheared from stone, the Eye flared again: a record of an erasure, the only testimony that could not be ledger-burned. The Cathedral does not trust it anymore. Neither does it dim.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Presence of the Ledger Church active within the city bounds. When bright, archives are being written—secrets are being recorded. When it fails, the Church's authority has fractured or turned inward. A player who reads this light knows when their name is being indexed, and whether that indexing serves as shield or knife.

## The Nail in Heaven (Rigel)

**Tier:** AncestorLight | **Spectral Class:** Frost

Low over the Toll Gate's eastern lip, where broken toll-chains litter the cistern below.

A Root-Pruner fixed this light the night she cut a corrupted vow-chain—the one the Toll-Saints had looped through a shopkeeper's wrist for three generations. When the link shattered, the impact struck upward: a blue nail drove through fog and pinned. Toll-Sisters read it as a prophecy. Free Graves read it as a precedent. Where it shines, vow-chains can still be broken. Where it moves, the method shifts.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Vow-chain structural integrity. A bright nail means chains can be severed; a dimmed one means they've learned to hide their weaknesses. When Frost-cold light reigns, imprisonment is precise and punishable. When it fails, bonds slacken—but also scatter. Tied to how the Anvil Covenant prices its break-tokens.

## The Widow's Ember (Betelgeuse)

**Tier:** TheForgotten | **Spectral Class:** TheForge

Western and failing, above the Descent Door's smoke at dusk. A small sun at the edge of extinction, burning the same color it has burned for a thousand years.

Widows say it carries the last warmth of a world that forgot its own widow-names. It has been dying since before the Cathedral was stone, and its death is not sudden. It burns not from rage but from inheritance—a star using up the last of what a world gave it. Where its light falls, old cloth still holds heat. When it finally fails, the Widow Courts have sworn they will stop accepting marriage-debts: there is no perpetuity, only drift toward cold.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Erasure progression made visible. Each dusk it is fractionally dimmer—a ledger written in light. A player tracking this light knows how much time remains before the Erasure takes what remains alive. The faster it fades in a run, the more the city has been hollowed. When it vanishes entirely, the final phase has begun.

## The Keeper's Lamp (Capella)

**Tier:** AncestorLight | **Spectral Class:** AskiyGold

Northwest above the Bellwright Forge, warming as the anvil cools. A golden anchor-light that settles into place each night like a vow kept.

A Bellwright hung this light as a covenant the first night she tempered honest steel: that hammered metal would never lie about its own weight, that a maker's hand would stay willing to strike true. The forge-master reads it as a ledger—bright nights mean the ore came clean, dim nights mean something rusted in the bone of things. When tools break for want of honest work, it dims not from distance but from broken oath. The Anvil Covenant keeps no excuses.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Resource crafting integrity; whether a forged tool will hold under weight or shatter under pressure. Bright = weapons remember their makers, Dim = the forge is compromising its vows. When this light burns true, Edge and Weight disciplines gain leverage. When it fades, even the best-made break under stress of the Erasure.

## The Second Witness (Procyon)

**Tier:** SpiritFire | **Spectral Class:** BoneStar

Rises after the Ledger's Eye, trails it eastward above Market Row. The companion-light that never comes first, never sits alone.

It follows the Ledger's Eye always—a second recording, a check against corruption. The Index Monks inscribed it as "that which records the record," but Free Graves know closer truth: it witnesses those who witness, sees who is watching what burns, remembers the rememberers. When a secret finally breaks the surface—when a name is spoken that the ledgers said was dead—this light brightens the moment before the voice sounds. The spoken word has already been seen.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Exposure of concealed truths and hidden mechanics. When it flares, the player's concealed actions become visible to the city—but only to those Grave-sworn to hold secrets. Bright = witnesses are gathering, Dim = cover still holds. A high-Breath discipline player reads this light to know when to speak and when to let the city guess.

## The Penitent's Return (Morning Star)

**Tier:** GuideStar | **Spectral Class:** Wanderer

The last to die before dawn, hangs over the Witness Rail. A wanderer that always returns, marking the threshold between night and settlement.

A debt-payer climbed the Cathedral bell-tower at the last hour before dawn, carrying a toll that could not be paid and a name that could not be given. They expected the rope. Instead, this light rose before them. It did not forgive the debt, but it marked the return to possibility. Now it appears before everyone who carries an unbearable toll—not to excuse, but to signal the moment between refusal and payment. Where it burns, old debts become negotiable.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Debt-settlement possibility opening and closing. Bright = the Widow Courts are accepting plea-debts, Dim = tolls only compound. This light gates access to non-violent debt resolution paths. When it burns high, mercy is credible. When it fails, debts can only be broken, not paid.

## The Nail Root (Polaris)

**Tier:** AncestorLight | **Spectral Class:** Frost

Fixed above the deep center, invisible unless you stand in the Under-Orchard's deepest chamber. It does not move; it grows downward.

It is not in the sky by accident—it grows downward through cold stone into the root-memory far below, pinned by the Ironroot's own heartbeat. No human light can reach it; only those who have died in the right place and returned know it is there. The Hollow Astronomers use its absolute stillness to read all motion below—every spiral a dead path takes, every route that opens in the grave-dark. It does not travel; it only remembers where everything else is traveling from.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Spirit-route stability and accessibility. Fixed and clear = Spirit passages are locked but knowable, Wobble or shadow = routes are shifting, new passages opening. A Glass-discipline player reads this light to navigate the deep realm. When it vanishes from sight entirely, major Spirit-structure has collapsed.

## The Bridge-Spark (Deneb)

**Tier:** TheForgotten | **Spectral Class:** TheDistant

Northeast, crosses above the Parish Shrine at the quarter-turn. A spark that burns at the boundary where two worlds hold their breath.

When the first Spirit-route opened, a light crossed from the living realm into the dead one, burning white at the moment of contact. It marks the thin place where two archways touch—the place where sound stops being echo and becomes conversation. Travelers lost between routes watch for it like a compass. When it sparks and holds, the path is open for passage. When it dims to nothing, the living and dead have parted further.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Spirit-realm accessibility and Spirit-death consequence severity. Bright = death carries passage into new routes, learning new things, Dim = death only locks routes further. This light shows whether the Erasure is sealing passages or opening new grave-routes. High brightness = Spirit-death is a strategic choice, low brightness = it's only punishment.

## The Falling Ash (Altair)

**Tier:** SpiritFire | **Spectral Class:** Meskanaw

Descends the southern arch, falls behind the Bell Pit's rim. A light in the act of falling, measuring descent like a sundial measures shame.

A cleanse-witness light that watched erasures complete for centuries. Each time a ledger is burned, each time a name is cut from stone, each time ash settles and the city breathes quiet again—it falls a fraction lower in the sky. It has fallen so far that some nights young people cannot see it. The Hollow Astronomers keep charts of its descent, measuring not the speed of falling but the total weight forgotten. Its fall is the city's chronicle written in light. It will not stop falling.

**Gameplay Whisper:** The weight of permanent erasure. Each major ledger-burn, each city-wide cleanse, drops this light noticeably lower. A player tracking it knows how much of the city has been forgotten in this run. When it reaches certain thresholds, the final Erasure-phase mechanics activate. It cannot be raised again.

## The Wound-Mark (Spica)

**Tier:** GuideStar | **Spectral Class:** BoneStar

Rises southeast, cuts through the humid air above the Desperate Camp's site. A sharp light, precise as the point of a blade.

It marks every place where a blade entered truth-work—where ledger-chains were severed, where vows were cut clean, where the Root-Pruner's knife was precise and the world was rewritten with cold certainty. It is blue because what it marks is too cold to bleed. When a chain breaks, it flares. When mercy breaks a chain instead of violence, it dims until the next wound is witnessed—and there is always a next wound. To watch it is to know where precision was paid for with blood.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Blade-work consequence recording. Bright = cuts are being witnessed, the world remembers your precise violence, Edge-discipline gains resonance, Dim = the city has stopped watching. When this light fails, violence becomes unmemoried—powerful, but also untethered from consequence. Tied directly to how Free Graves read the world.

## The Harp-String Scar (Vega)

**Tier:** AncestorLight | **Spectral Class:** Wisakedjak

High zenith, sings above all roofs at the dark's deepest hour. A light that resonates on frequencies the ear cannot place.

When the Hollow Astronomers first mapped Spirit-song and tried to find its root, they plucked a chord from the Erasure's own hum and it fractured upward, visible as a scar across the black. It sings frequencies no human voice can reach, but the rootless can feel it in their bones—a vibration that matches something forgotten inside. When deep work happens, when the Metronome strikes its strongest beat, it hums a harmony that was never supposed to have a voice. The sound of it is the color of sorrow learning to carry itself.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Deep mechanical resonance and the Metronome's influence. When it hums, the 120Hz pulse is strongest—time-locked mechanics activate, the city's heartbeat becomes audible. A player deep in Root or Ash discipline reads this light to time their heaviest work. When it falls silent, the Metronome has weakened; the Erasure is dampening even the sky's vibration.

## The Wanderer's Mark (Arcturus)

**Tier:** TheForgotten | **Spectral Class:** Wanderer

Wanders the north-arc, reds the Cathedral's stones at season-turn. A light that moves because it must, carrying a grief that will not rest.

An Anvil Covenant oath-bearer who refused to stay anchored, who walked seasons looking for the wife whose name the Ledger Church had erased. The oath-bond would not let her rest; she rose into sky instead, orange and aching, tracing the same path across the year. She moves because the vow compels; she burns because she remembers. Where she is in the sky, change is possible—new seasons crack old walls, old debts become negotiable. Where she departs, winter locks in.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Seasonal cycle and faction pressure rotation. As this light wanders through the year, different factions pressure harder or ease their grip. When it's warm-orange, the Widow Courts soften and healing is possible. When it cools, the Toll-Saints press every debt. A 100-hour run can be mapped by where this light is in its journey. Where it is, that is what season the city is in.

## The Debt-Seven (Pleiades)

**Tier:** SpiritFire | **Spectral Class:** Wisakedjak

Clustered low over the eastern terrace-wall, where the Under-Orchard's branches break through ledger-stone. Seven lights that speak in the language of broken contracts.

Seven debts made one covenant, and seven witnesses to a single failure. When they cluster tight, the debt is still unbroken—seven lights, one descending chord of judgment. When they scatter in the sky, it means one witness has been found and turned, one angle of judgment opened, one reason to refuse the entire system. The Free Graves watch their spacing like a ledger written in stars they can read from the ground. Each night, they pass word: how many points are still aligned?

**Gameplay Whisper:** Covenant integrity and faction witness status. Tight cluster = the unifying debt still holds power, Scattered = one faction has been compromised. This light gates access to breaking major covenants. When tight, Anvil vows cannot be reversed. When scattered, even the strongest Toll-Saint chain can be questioned. Tied directly to how the Free Graves record and undermine institutional power.

## The Toll-Draw (Big Dipper)

**Tier:** GuideStar | **Spectral Class:** TheForge

The circumpolar draw, dips toward the Forge where debts are reforged into tools, rises toward the north where debts are written into root-vows. The shape of measure itself, moving but returning.

It is the shape of the tool that draws toll-water from the deep cistern—that first draw before the first payment, the exact measure of what is owed. It dips toward the Bellwright Forge where what is owed gets reforged into weapons and armor; it rises toward the north where what is owed gets stitched into vows that hold for generations. To watch it is to know: every toll is a measurement, and every measurement is an oath. It does not hide its necessity; it only circles like the seasons, dipping and rising, drawing and pouring, again.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Toll economy and price-setting. Bright and deep-drawing = tolls are high and strictly enforced, Mara's pricing favors the Church, Dim and shallow = tolls can be negotiated, Free Guilds have leverage. This light shows whether the player can escape toll-debt through negotiation or must break it through force. When it draws deep, Weight-discipline becomes necessary.

## The Chained Kin (Andromeda Galaxy)

**Tier:** AncestorLight | **Spectral Class:** TheDistant

Far northwest, visible only from the highest bell-tower or the deepest grave-mine, a pale prisoner beyond the edge of the mapped world. Closer than it appears; farther than anyone dares measure.

A kingdom that refused to accept the Erasure, that tried to record itself in light instead of ledger, tried to keep every name alive through pure defiance. It remains far away, bound by its own resistance—a prisoner of its own refusal. The Hollow Astronomers say it shows what happens when an entire world tries to keep itself alive in sky rather than yield to silence. It is proof that the Erasure reaches beyond Veyrholt. It is a warning we cannot yet read, or else we would have already fallen.

**Gameplay Whisper:** External threat progression and world-state consequences. This light becomes more visible as the player escalates the conflict—more visible = the world is watching, larger forces are aware, the ending is now contested outside the city. When fully visible, the final paths open. When it vanishes entirely, Veyrholt has sealed itself from the outside, and all endings become internal.

## The River Recorded (Milky Way)

**Tier:** TheForgotten | **Spectral Class:** Meskanaw

The arch spanning north to south, the recorded river running through the sky—not fixed, but flowing down through all marked places, carrying ash-dust and the light-weight of a thousand broken names.

It is the great river made of all the light-dust that fell from erased histories, burned ledgers, and names the Ledger Church decided could not continue. It flows from north to south, the path every lost ledger takes, every administrative dissolution follows, every record eventually pours into. To read the Milky Way is to read all the things Veyrholt has chosen to forget—not as words, but as the accumulated weight of light, the specific gravity of grief. It does not shine; it accumulates. And it flows in only one direction: down.

**Gameplay Whisper:** Archive integrity and ledger-permanence. When bright, recorded memory survives—the daemon's own ledger cannot be burned. When dim, the Erasure is winning; even your personal records become dust. The river's brightness shows whether what you do will be remembered or forgotten by the world. When it runs dry entirely, all memory is lost, and the final Erasure has won.

## How the Hollow Astronomers Read the Sky

They call it correspondence: each light carries a frequency, each position holds a meaning, each absence speaks louder than any presence. They map the sky not as humans map territory, but as the deaf read vibration—through pressure, duration, and the gaps between pulses.

To read is to establish a covenant: the Astronomer offers the sky their attention, and the sky offers the Astronomer a route. Not prophecy. Not guidance. Route-knowledge: where the Spirit-realm presses close to the living one, where a death in the right place yields passage to the right information, where what is forgotten here has accumulated there and can be recovered. They also know how to break their reading: how to misread the positions, how to invert the meanings, how to teach the sky a lie so profound it becomes true. The Index Monks fear this above all things—not the reading, but the breaking. A Hollow Astronomer who chooses to corrupt their own charts can hide an entire faction from the ledgers' sight.

## The Thirteenth Moon's Covenant

It is not among the sixteen lights, and this is by oath. The Thirteenth Moon is the only thing that remembers what the lights have forgotten—the only thing that witnesses the witnesses. It does not speak, does not guide, does not shine. It watches.

When the Erasure first came, the moon could have fallen inward to the Ironroot, could have surrendered its light like the stars did. Instead, it chose to become dark—to hide, to refuse the ledger-burning that would have recorded it. The sixteen lights are all recorded, all mapped, all owned by the factions. The Thirteenth Moon owns nothing and no one. It is the refusal made visible: a void in the sky that proves light is not the only form a covenant can take. To the Free Graves, its darkness is the purest testimony. To everyone else, it is simply absent.

## The Watcher's Evening Rite

In Thornhaven, when the light fails and the Metronome begins its 10-second breath, a local—any local, any watcher—stands on their terrace and looks up. No words. No incense. No offering. Only a counting.

One breath: the Ledger's Eye bright or dimmed. Two breaths: the Nail in Heaven's position; is it moving or still? Three: the Keeper's Lamp warm or cooling. Four: the River—is it flowing or clogged? They count the sixteen, one heart-beat between each, until the dark is complete. Then they descend into their dwelling and inscribe that night's reading into their own small ledger—the one the Ledger Church will never see, the one they share with the Free Graves by leaving it where it will be found by someone they trust. This is not prayer. It is testimony: I was here. I watched. I remember what was light tonight. What is here is recorded. I refute the silence.
