# NPCs & Faction Clocks — Thornhaven Terrace

## Ledger Church

### Toll-Sister Vey
**Oath-Name**: The Arbiter's Tongue | **Faction**: Ledger Church
**Wants**: To prove the spouse-name erased was *necessary*, not cruel. **Fears**: That mercy and ledger law are incompatible.
**Knows**: The exact moment of erasure; the clerk who signed it; the ledger page it was inked on; whether the death-tithe was debt or ritual.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Nods at the Toll Gate, speaks only the toll amount; no eye contact.
- *Trusted*: Offers a worn cup of water; shares the church bell schedule; names the day the erasure came down.
- *Crossed*: Demands the name be re-entered into the ledger by your hand, or refuses to sound the bell for your next death.

**Dialogue Sample**: "I tell you the price of the gate, and you tell me you loved the person who passed through it. But the ledger does not hold love—it holds debt. So which is true? Neither. Both are incomplete until you stop paying, and *then* the ledger will be forced to read what *you* say it means." *(Rakugo logic-flip: the apparent contradiction resolves only when the player chooses to rewrite the ledger's reading, not just its entries.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "What is the bell's name?" | **Close** — "I will scrub your record myself."

**React To**: Debt Heat — if player breaks tolls, Vey marks the church bell cracked (visual): future tolls cost more. Mercy Ledger — if player shows mercy to debtors, Vey begins the slow work of re-entering small names in the margins.

### Morrow
**Oath-Name**: The Grave-Light Keeper | **Faction**: Ledger Church (caretaker, not member)
**Wants**: To remain lit. To remain remembered. *Posthumously*: to become the route-anchor the player cannot reach alone.
**Fears**: Being forgotten. Being re-erased. That the grave-lantern's name will never be spoken again.
**Knows**: Every name buried in the grave-orchard; the days they died; the names of those who mourned them; which roots are oldest; where the deepest water runs.

**Standing Ladder**: (Only accessible as a Spirit-route memory or through echoes the player learns)
- *Stranger*: A silhouette behind the grave-lantern's flame; warmth, never words.
- *Trusted*: Speaks. Gives direction. Warns of coming cleanses. Weaves small roots into protective markers.
- *Crossed*: Silent. The light extinguishes. The roots tangle to bar all paths forward.

**Dialogue Sample**: "You ask why I did not fight. Because the law was written before the rain fell, and rain does not break contracts—it only makes the ink run. I chose to let the ink spread instead. Read what bleeds." *(The grief becomes legible through damage, not through words.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "The grave-lantern is still burning." | **Close** — "The grave is empty now."

**React To**: Environmental Damage — if the grave-lantern is restored or kindled anew, Morrow's presence brightens; if destroyed, becomes a fixed point the player must spiral around. Name Fragments — Morrow's echoes strengthen as the spouse-name is recovered.

### The Mason of Grey Orchard
**Oath-Name**: Stone-Reader Kess | **Faction**: Ledger Church (apprentice mason, bound by debt)
**Wants**: To be forgiven. To build something that will not be erased. To prove that stone can hold a name better than ledger.
**Fears**: Being hanged for the cleanse. That the next mason will chisel their own name next.
**Knows**: Which grave-stones are authentic; where false names are buried; how to read the chisel-marks that denote hurried erasures; the sound that stone makes when a name is hammered out.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Refuses eye contact; works with head down; may slip a chalk mark on your sleeve meaning "unsafe, watch the stairs."
- *Trusted*: Whispers true names of graves; offers tools; begins to believe you might survive what's coming.
- *Crossed*: Disappears. The graves crack overnight. New false names are inscribed.

**Dialogue Sample**: "You ask me which names I carved and which I was forced to erase. The stone remembers better than I do. Every blow of the chisel—that was me. Every erasure—that was *me* too. So I am both the mason and the breach. Do you forgive both of me, or neither?" *(Rakugo: confession reframes the sin as structural rather than moral; forgiveness becomes about accepting incompleteness.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "Show me the stone with no name." | **Close** — "I will carve your grave before you ask."

**React To**: Environmental Damage — if grave-stones are vandalized, Kess vanishes. Mercy Ledger — if player spares Kess, Kess begins secretly restoring grave-names with borrowed stone.

### Pulse-Keeper Carn
**Oath-Name**: The Chain-Counter | **Faction**: Toll-Saints
**Wants**: To hear the toll-chain's rhythm unbroken, one thousand times in a lifetime. To be the witness that counts them.
**Fears**: Silence. A chain with a missing link. Being forgotten before their count ends.
**Knows**: Every toll paid by every person; which debts are ancient and which are new; the sound each person's coin makes; whether you carry toll-weight or fresh copper.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Counts in a flat voice; does not look up.
- *Trusted*: Hums the toll-rhythm; shares a coin that sings; confesses they have been here so long they don't remember if they are counting others or being counted.
- *Crossed*: Counts you as dead; refuses to hear new coins; their chant becomes a knell.

**Dialogue Sample**: "You want to know how many times the chain rings. I will tell you: enough times that I became the chain. So when you pass through, I do not move it—you move *me*. This is not cruelty. This is the truest toll, and you pay it simply by existing in a place where the bell knows your name." *(Rakugo: the apparent punishment is revealed as mutual entanglement.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "Count me fairly." | **Close** — "You did not pay."

**React To**: Debt Heat — if tolls are broken, Carn's voice cracks; the chain becomes erratic. Faction Leverage — if player gains Free Guilds backing, Carn becomes conflicted; may offer a hidden path that bypasses tolls.

## Index Monks

### Index Clerk Oth
**Oath-Name**: The Archivist | **Faction**: Index Monks
**Wants**: To find the true name of the spouse hidden in back-dated ledgers. To prove that remembering is a form of power.
**Fears**: That the name was never real; that it exists in margins and blank pages only.
**Knows**: Every version of the spouse-name that was ever recorded; the handwriting of every scribe who touched it; which ledgers are the oldest copies; where Index forgeries hide.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Gestures to the reading room; speaks only in ledger categories.
- *Trusted*: Shares a page with margins intact; whispers the scribe's name; shows you how to read between the lines.
- *Crossed*: Seals the archives. Rewrites provenance. Declares your spouse-name administratively fictional.

**Dialogue Sample**: "You ask me to find a name. But names are not found—they are *chosen* as true by enough voices speaking together. I have found seventeen versions of the name you seek. All are true. All are lies. Which one will you fight for, and what cost will you pay when the others claim you are erasing *them*?" *(Rakugo: the quest for truth inverts into a choice to impose truth.*)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "What was their true name?" | **Close** — "There is no record."

**React To**: Name Fragments — each fragment recovered alters Index Monks' records; Oth becomes either ally or adversary based on what the player does with the knowledge. Spirit Route Knowledge — if player learns how to access the Deep Ledger, Oth becomes a critical gatekeeper.

### Illumina, the Margin-Keeper
**Oath-Name**: The Silent Scribe | **Faction**: Index Monks
**Wants**: To write in the margins; to preserve what the official ledgers forbid. To keep one record that cannot be audited.
**Fears**: That the margins will be erased too. That silence is the only safe ink left.
**Knows**: Every administrative erasure; the names of all the forbidden ledgers; where the censored pages are hidden; how to read a blank page and know what was there.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Writes but does not speak; leaves a blank page where a warning should be.
- *Trusted*: Teaches you the cipher for margin-text; gives you a ledger of your own; swears to keep it safe.
- *Crossed*: Writes your name in the margins marked for erasure.

**Dialogue Sample**: "Everyone fears the blank page. But I learned long ago: the blank page is a mirror. Write in the margins, and the page will show you who you are afraid to claim. Does that terrify you? Then you are ready." *(Rakugo: fear becomes a diagnostic tool.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "Write in the margin." | **Close** — "The margins are sealed."

**React To**: Environmental Damage — if ledgers are burned, Illumina's margins become the only record left; Illumina becomes invaluable. Shadow Scar Map — Illumina can read Shadow scars as margin-text; offers to document the player's Shadow.

## Anvil Covenant

### Oath-Smith Dern
**Oath-Name**: Kept-Memory | **Faction**: Anvil Covenant
**Wants**: To forge a weapon that remembers the user's name. To create proof that violence need not erase.
**Fears**: That Kept Quiet (the old pruning knife) was a masterwork they could never match. That they are apprentice to a ghost.
**Knows**: How Kept Quiet was forged; the smith who made it; whether the blade remembers its wielder or only its cuts; what sound a true oath-weapon makes.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Eyes the player's hands; assesses their grip; offers no credit.
- *Trusted*: Shares the forge-heat; tells you the story of Kept Quiet's making; begins a weapon for you, slowly.
- *Crossed*: Refuses to hammer for you; offers instead to re-forge your weapon into something useless.

**Dialogue Sample**: "You ask me if the old knife remembers the person who held it or the people it cut. The answer is both, which is why a true oath-weapon is a curse. Every stroke you make with it, it remembers. And one day, when it has remembered enough, it will decide whether to cut the world or cut your own hand." *(Rakugo: mastery inverts into being mastered by the tool.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "Teach me what Kept Quiet taught you." | **Close** — "That knife is ash now."

**React To**: Faction Leverage — if player allies with Ledger Church, Dern becomes guarded; if allied with Free Graves, Dern opens the deeper forge. Shadow Scar Map — Dern can read Shadow scars into the blade; a weapon forged with Shadow-understanding becomes doubly sharp.

### Hammer-Chorus (Collective)
**Oath-Name**: The Synchronized Blow | **Faction**: Anvil Covenant
**Wants**: To forge something that no single hand could make. To prove that violence can be collective without being mob.
**Fears**: That individual blame will be assigned to a collective act. That one weak hammer will break the rhythm.
**Knows**: The schedule of the forge's loudest hours; which weapons are comissioned by which factions; what breaking an oath-weapon sounds like (a note that cannot unhear itself).

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: You hear the hammers but see no individual. The rhythm is the greeting.
- *Trusted*: One hammer-bearer breaks rhythm to speak with you; reveals their name and the weapon they carry.
- *Crossed*: Every hammer falls at once with a note like a shattered bell.

**Dialogue Sample**: "You ask which hammer struck the fatal blow. We are fourteen. We all did. Will you punish all of us, or none? Either way, the rhythm continues." *(Rakugo: moral clarity dissolves into systemic complicity.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "Forge with us." | **Close** — "The hammer has forgotten your name."

**React To**: Debt Heat — if player kills anvil-workers, the remaining Chorus becomes fragmented and unpredictable; future forges take longer. Mercy Ledger — if player shows mercy to a worker, that worker breaks rhythm once to thank them.

## Widow Courts

### Widow's Advocate Sael
**Oath-Name**: The Inheritance Keeper | **Faction**: Widow Courts
**Wants**: To determine what the spouse-name should be: property, memory, person, or void. To hold the deciding opinion.
**Fears**: That the answer is void. That the spouse's absence is the only truth that remains.
**Knows**: The legal status of the spouse-name under Widow law; whether the marriage was recorded or was itself erased; what claim a widow or widower actually has; whether you can inherit grief as legal property.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Offers condolences written in formal script; speaks only in legal terms.
- *Trusted*: Sets aside the formal register; tells you what the law should say instead of what it does; sides with you in private.
- *Crossed*: Declares the spouse-name property of the state; confiscates whatever memory you carry.

**Dialogue Sample**: "Marriage is a vow written in two languages: one in words, one in law. The law has no ears for the words you heard. So I must ask you: was the person your spouse because the ledger said so, or in spite of it? If you answer 'despite,' then you own nothing, and the law has no claim on you. But you will carry this alone." *(Rakugo: freedom inverts into isolation.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "I will defend the marriage." | **Close** — "The vow is void."

**React To**: Name Fragments — as player recovers fragments of the spouse-name, Sael's legal opinion shifts; final judgment depends on how much of the name is recovered. Mercy Ledger — if player shows mercy to widows and widowers in the world, Sael becomes an unexpected ally.

## Free Graves

### Keeper Moth (Oath-Name: The Unmarked Guide)
**Oath-Name**: The Unmarked Guide | **Faction**: Free Graves
**Wants**: To hand the blank Book to someone who will use it to refuse. To create proof that a life can be lived outside ledger law.
**Fears**: That the Book will be stolen and re-ledgered. That no one can truly refuse the system.
**Knows**: Which graves have no names; where the Free Graves hideouts are; how to forge absence; what it means to be a ghost before you die.

**Standing Ladder**:
- *Stranger*: Tends graves in silence; may leave a stone pointing toward an un-marked path.
- *Trusted*: Gives you the blank Book; teaches you the ritual command `OPEN BOOK <name>`; swears to keep your name out of all records.
- *Crossed*: Knows you betrayed the name; hides from you; the graves become trackless.

**Dialogue Sample**: "They say the Book is empty. But you and I know better. An empty book is not nothing—it is *permission*. Permission to define yourself by what you *do*, not what you *owe*. Would you dare to keep that permission, or will you fill it with words before you even begin?" *(Rakugo: emptiness becomes agency.)*

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "I reject the ledger." | **Close** — "You recorded your name yourself."

**React To**: Debt Heat — if player breaks tolls, Moth offers shelter. Faction Leverage — if any faction learns where Moth is, Moth disappears and must be found again through Spirit routes.

**Words That Matter**: **Open** — "Plant something with me." | **Close** — "Your seeds will not grow here."

**React To**: Environmental Damage — if the grave-orchard is burned, Orchid reveals a hidden seed-vault. Mercy Ledger — if player protects children and refugees, Orchid trusts them with deeper garden secrets.

## Faction Clocks — Terrace Behavior

### Ledger Church Clock
**What advancing looks like**: Church bells crack audibly (descending pitch, higher freq breaking off); grave-tax fees inscribed in public ledger walls; masonry crews mark new graves for "verification audit"; sermons shift from mercy language to debt language; clergy wear black wax seals on their wrists (one more seal per advancement).

**Seeds into next run**: Clergy memory is longer; they recognize faces from previous runs; new graves start with pre-written debt annotations; the Bell Pit's "alive count" descends faster.

---

### Toll-Saints Clock
**What advancing looks like**: Toll-chains grow heavier (audible difference); toll-gates rise higher; Pulse-Keepers station themselves at more routes; the rhythm shifts to a minor key; toll-prices update on public stones daily instead of seasonally.

**Seeds into next run**: The rhythmic toll becomes a haunting presence; tolls are collected even in Spirit routes; new coins are rejected as "unrecognized weight."

---

### Index Monks Clock
**What advancing looks like**: Ledger pages go blank in public archives (information restricted); new scribes appear wearing unmarked robes (censoring crew); the illumination of margin-texts becomes a crime; scholars whisper instead of speaking; back-dated ledger entries show revised provenance.

**Seeds into next run**: Previous run's records are re-written; Spirit routes are mapped differently; the player's own history becomes legally disputed.

---

### Free Guilds Market Clock (Mara's Price-Setting)
**What advancing looks like**: Market goods become scarce (prices climb); Free Guilds traders disappear into hidden routes; Thornhaven markets contract into smaller circles; barter replaces coin; Mara is nowhere to be found, only her price-marks appear.

**Seeds into next run**: Traders remember the player's reputation; prices reflect previous debts or favors; new traders arrive only if reputation is high enough.

---

## Cross-Faction Eruption Spiral

**Tension state**: When multiple clocks advance simultaneously (e.g., Ledger Church + Toll-Saints + Index Monks all advance), faction conflict erupts visibly:
- Clergy and Toll-keepers argue at intersections.
- Monks produce "counter-ledgers" claiming prior versions are false.
- Free Graves traders vanish entirely.
- Bell Pit's count becomes chaotic; the Thirteen Bells Warden stirs earlier than expected.

**Player agency**: Advancing one clock slower than others creates leverage — factions compete for player favor. Breaking one clock entirely invites the others to hunt together (but briefly: shared enemies don't last).
