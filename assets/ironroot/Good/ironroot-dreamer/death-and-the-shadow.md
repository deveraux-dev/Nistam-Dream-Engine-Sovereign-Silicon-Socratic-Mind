# Death and the Shadow: The Root-Cycle, the Tombstone, and the Lineage

## The Root-Cycle Endings

Death is not the game's ending — it is a state shift. Three types close the Root-Cycle:

**Return** is the cleanest exit. The ledger banks your records at the Witness Rail — the stone platform where testimony is verified before it becomes permanent. Returning is felt as *permission to rest*. XP keeps. Mercy ledger keeps. Shadow scars remain. The spouse-name inches closer by one verified witness. It feels like breathing again after holding your breath too long.

**Death** opens the Spirit side. You die beyond the city's reach — in a locked room, a sealed passage, a Spirit's own mirror-chamber. The living body is left behind; the incarnation descends into the terrace's reversed half. Returning means passage through the Spirit Forest Mirror, a route the dying learn by repetition. Death strengthens the Shadow (one new scar, one learned habit). Each death teaches the city an access path it did not have before. It feels like drowning in ash, then waking lighter — the cost paid in forgetting; the route purchased by suffering.

**Erasure** is the worst ending. The ledger is struck, the body unmade, the name severed. No return platform materializes. No Spirit route opens. Only a **Tombstone** remains — an un-deletable monument that marks a permanent hole in the world. The next incarnation awakens without memory, chained to the dead's accumulated debts, forever haunted by what they carried but cannot claim. It feels like being born into a debt that is already overdue.

## Spirit Traversal: The Terrace's Dead Half

The Spirit Forest Mirror is the terrace as it exists when the living are ash. The same rooms; the same walls; all warmth removed. Passages mirror the living city but reversed — what was closed is open; what was open is locked. Entering from the living side requires dying in a specific room. The Spirit reaches out only where death has touched before.

Rules for the corpse-run:
- A Spirit walk ends only at living return gates (the Parish Shrine, the Under-Orchard's Root Cellar, the Witness Rail).
- Carrying a message from the dead to the living costs one living memory.
- A locked living room may be crossed only from the dead side — its threshold sealed from life, open from death.
- Shadows cast longer in the terrace; your silhouette moves one beat ahead of your body.

Three death-gated secrets in the MVP terrace:

1. **Grave-Mine Descent** — Die in the Under-Orchard's Toll Drain. The descent path exists only as Spirit; it leads to the buried ledger-vaults where merchants hide unpaid debts. Return alive with one vault name spoken = leverage for bargaining with the Toll-Saints.

2. **The Locked Shrine Room** — Die at the Parish Shrine's altar. The sealed chamber behind it, inaccessible from life, holds the handwritten rule-book the Ledger Church uses to audit dreams. Read one page = one question you may later ask the Index Monks, verbatim, and they must answer.

3. **The Ash Orchard** — Die within Grey Orchard's memory-stone circle. In Spirit, the orchard blooms in reverse — burned branches grow live fire; the graves open and release their names as ash-motes. Gather three name-fragments = one piece of the spouse-name recovered, permanently.

## Tombstones: The Monuments

A Tombstone is a failure permanent as stone. Every death that ends in erasure leaves one.

What they look like: a **cracked ceramic bell** inverted over a nail ring. The bell is unglazed, split at the rim, filled with rain-weight and ash. The nail ring holds seven iron nails driven at full force — they cannot be pulled. The whole stands eight spans high, weathered, colder than the surrounding stones. It does not rot or fade. It cannot be moved, melted, or erased by mortal hands.

What they do: they break line-of-sight for what hunts the deep. Any Spirit in the terrace that would cross a Tombstone's path must move a wide circle. Tombstones render into the world for every keeper on the relay — a message that something here dies every time. Three Tombstones in one sector = the sector is locked; no new keeper may die there until two are witnessed.

Etiquette: a keeper may lay flowers, ash-cloth, or a carved name-chip at another's Tombstone, but only after resting at it for a full Metronome-breath. Defacing a Tombstone marks the defacer's own name for erasure. The city watches.

## The SoulWord Lineage

Every keeper carries a **SoulWord** — sixty-four bytes of identity, hashed and sealed to each death-cycle. It is not a save file; it is a ledger line that proves the chain.

What persists diegetically after death:
- XP learned (the body's muscle memory — technique stays, reflexes keep; *the ledger keeps what you learned*).
- Debts incurred (the city remembers who you owe).
- Scars earned (the Shadow inherits them, records them, wears them as proof).
- Routes discovered (death teaches the world new paths; the world does not un-learn).

An unbroken ancestral chain — a lineage where each SoulWord in sequence carries the previous one's hash — means different things to different powers. The Free Graves recognize unbroken chains as *proof of resistance* (you survived long enough to pass it forward). The Ledger Church recognizes them as *proof of debt* (each ancestor adds interest; you inherit the compound obligation). An orphaned SoulWord — one with no parent hash — awakens the Widow Courts' claim: property of the state, a vessel to be rented.

The fresh incarnation quickens *by the sealed mark* — a touch of ash to the forehead at the Tombstone's base. No memory returns. No debts forgive. Only the pull forward, inexorable: *the ledger knows what you learned; the body knows what it suffered; only the mind is blank.*

## The Shadow in the Terrace

### Hour 0: the Scripted Death at the Desperate Camp

The first Shadow appears not as a choice but as the world's answer. The player dies at the Desperate Camp — overwhelmed, scripted, the world's weight made lesson. At death, something in the player's own silhouette goes wrong — it *lingers*. The stain spreads like ink through water, taking the shape of exhaustion and refusal. It is the player, recorded.

### Stalker Behavior

The Shadow watches method, not morality. It observes:
- Where the player stands in combat (it learns position bias).
- How the player escapes (it studies dodge rhythm).
- What the player refuses to do (mercy patterns, unused tools, chains unbroken).
- Where the player dies repeatedly (it nests in failure sites).

It inherits scars **from every event**:
- Tollroad: chain-burn marks, the shape of desperation.
- Cinder Parish: ash-crack geometry, edges splintered.
- Widow Canal: waterline scars, something drowned in the silhouette.
- Anvil Ward: nail rings pressed into the phantom flesh.

### Three Paths — Encounter Samples

When the Shadow reaches **Blighted** tier, three doors open in one fight:

1. **Reject** — *"You are the audit. We are the measure. Let us count your failures."*
The Shadow becomes a combat test. It copies your technique but faster, punishing repetition. Every attack you use twice in succession costs stagger-lock. Every parry-timing you repeat ends in a counter-grab. This is mastery: it forces improvisation or death. Killing it leaves you alone in the arena, exhausted, cleaner. It may return at Harbinger tier; if it does, it will have learned all you taught it before.

2. **Integrate** — *"You are seen. What you did is written. What you refuse is louder than what you claim."*
The Shadow becomes a witness. It speaks back what you did: *the mercy you granted one debtor but not the next, the official you killed for justice but spared for fear, the ledger you burned but carried*. This is testimony: it exposes the gap between your vows and your actions. Accepting it gives it a voice in the relays — it may speak for you at the Widow Courts, may testify for your mercy, may betray your cruelties. It stays, grows, shadows your choices. It is the player as remembered habit.

3. **Embrace Void** — *"We are the Rorschach blot now. Every target is legible. Every heart is visible. Let us read together."*
The Shadow merges. The stain rises into your shape completely. You become a two-silhouette — impossible geometry, seen-and-unseen at once. Every enemy in range becomes *exposed* — their debts visible, their fears readable, their defeat certain. Combat becomes execution. But the cost is highest: memory begins to grey. *Why* you did each thing fades; only *what* you did remains. Endings change around this choice; the city treats you differently.

## The Cozy Counterweight

Death in Ironroot would be only weight without the hearth. After erasure, after a hard cycle, one room always opens: a **space of rest**, diegetic and earned.

It contains:
- A **cracked ceramic bowl** filled with warm ash — hand-shaped, ancient, the only surface that doesn't hurt.
- A **candle cairn** — small stones stacked, each one lit by a keeper before. Light stacks here; the room grows warmer with each visit.
- **Folded cloth** — dyed black, held in layers, soft enough to hold without flinching.
- **A low flame**, barely moving, burning at the edge of what sound can reach.

It is the only place where the Shadow does not follow. It is the only place where the spouse-name is *not* a wound — just a remembered warmth, held, kept.

Sitting here, the player plants the grave-orchard back to light. Slowly. Every rest-cycle adds one root, one leaf, one small fire. Years of patience, in-world, become a counterweight to the violence above: *the world may hurt you, but here you may rest; and from rest, another cycle can begin.*

## Measurement: Death Sites → Forward Motion

- **The Desperate Camp** (scripted death) → Route: Spirit Forest passage + Shadow manifestation (Stalker tier; the choice it seeds gates future paths)
- **Parish Shrine altar** (death-gated) → Scar + Route: the Locked Shrine Room; one binding question owed by the Index Monks; a Shadow scar (the watermark of entering the sacred, corrupted)
- **Toll Drain** (death-gated) → Route: Grave-Mine descent; merchant vault leverage against the Toll-Saints
- **Grey Orchard memory-stone circle** (death-gated) → Fragments: the Ash Orchard; three ash-motes = one spouse-name fragment, permanent
- **Tollroad** (repeat deaths) → Witness: a Toll-Sister flinches, records, begins to question orders
- **Hearth / rest-site** (chosen return) → Motion: the grave-orchard grows; the next cycle begins lighter
