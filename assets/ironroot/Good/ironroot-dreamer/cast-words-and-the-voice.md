# Cast-Words and the Voice: Ironroot's Language of Pressure and Sound

## The Glyph-Word Grammar

A **glyph** is a struck frequency given a mark—the smallest unit of casting. It is not a letter; it is a *moment of pressure made sonic*. When you speak a cast-word, you are striking a sequence of these moments into the world's ledger, one per turn. The glyph itself has no meaning until it lands; meaning is *what it expects the world to do next*.

A **cast-sentence** chains glyphs into a temporal shape. Each glyph occupies exactly one action-turn. The sentence's *length in glyphs is its cost in vigor, ash residue, or nosebleeds*—there is no abstraction. Casting "Ember" (1 glyph) costs less than a four-glyph working, and a player feels this in the body: the longer the sentence, the deeper the toll. Interruption cuts the sentence mid-word; an incomplete cast-word does *not* fail safely—it completes wrong. A severed glyph scatters its intent into the ledger as **shattered will**: a Recorded failure that other factions can later weaponize, or that leaves scar-tissue the Shadow can read.

The world only accepts *complete cast-sentences*. A speaker must commit: start the glyphs or do not speak. This is the discipline that separates a whisper from a chant.

## The Sixteen Cast-Words: Two per Oath Discipline

### Edge (Precision & Execution)

**Severance** — 2 glyphs — *"The cloth breaks where it is sewn."*
Cut through one bound target or loop-seal. Inscribed edges glow grave-iron clean.
Interrupted: the loop tightens instead. Target grapples gain +1 favor.

**Clean Mercy** — 3 glyphs — *"The knife remembers when to stop."*
Non-lethal execution: target falls unconscious instead of death. Ledger reads "Spared."
Interrupted: blade turns; the caster takes half the damage instead, inscribed in flesh.

### Weight (Hyper-Armor & Refusal)

**Anchor** — 1 glyph — *"I plant."*
Caster cannot be moved or thrown for one exchange. Armor briefly turns stone-grey.
Interrupted: caster immobilized until the next turn; attacker gains +1 position.

**Break Stone Vow** — 4 glyphs — *"This gate was built to break. I will finish the work."*
Destroy one sealed door, jammed mechanism, or faction gate lock. Leaves rubble the Shadow can hide in.
Interrupted: gate locks tighter. Ledger inscribes the failed break as a debt owed to the Ledger Church.

### Breath (Silence, Feints, Interruption)

**Whisper Vent** — 2 glyphs — *"I take back what was heard."*
Steal one recent testimony from an NPC's active memory. They forget they said it; the ledger reads "Censored."
Interrupted: the vent misfires; caster accidentally speaks the testimony aloud to all present.

**Ritual Silence** — 3 glyphs — *"The bells stop ringing for three heartbeats."*
Toll-Saint chants cannot sound; faction gates pause their countdown. Time does not advance for timed events in this zone.
Interrupted: bells ring *backward*—previous events replay as phantoms. Reality becomes unstable.

### Thread (Binding, Traps, Non-Lethal)

**Tether Anchor** — 2 glyphs — *"You carry what you took from me."*
Bind one target within sight to a location. They cannot leave; each turn they attempt to move costs vigor. Lasts until the knot is cut.
Interrupted: tether snaps and wraps the caster instead. Caster is bound; target flees.

**Stitch Platform** — 4 glyphs — *"The cloth remembers the shape of a bridge. Build it again."*
Create a temporary platform or spanning thread across a gap. Dissolves after three crossings or one minute. Leaves behind faint luminescent scars.
Interrupted: platform tears mid-crossing. Climbers fall; the torn thread becomes a trap that catches the next creature.

### Ash (Damage-Over-Time, Area Denial, Purge)

**Ember Seed** — 1 glyph — *"Burn."*
Throw ash-coal into an area. Enemies standing there take 1 pressure per turn; the ground smolders for three turns.
Interrupted: coal extinguishes uselessly. Caster is marked as "failed fire-keeper."

**Seal Burn** — 3 glyphs — *"Ash into the ledger's ink. Burn the record."*
Destroy one written contract, vow-suture, or official seal by setting it aflame. Ledger reads the destruction as "irreversible cleansing." Recovered Name Fragments within the seal are lost forever.
Interrupted: seal becomes ash-bonded (indestructible). Caster gains Tarnish +1 for the failed profanity.

### Root (Sustain, Growth, Anchor)

**Root Hold** — 2 glyphs — *"I take hold in deep earth."*
Caster enters a grounded state; cannot be moved, but also cannot move or attack. Slowly regenerates vigor. Lasts until willingly released.
Interrupted: roots grow inward instead of outward. Caster takes pressure damage and is slowed for two turns.

**Resurrect the Passage** — 4 glyphs — *"The bell vein remembers. Show me what was buried."*
Reopen one sealed or collapsed passage. Reveal a former route hidden by ruin or administrative erasure. Cost: deep nosebleed, vision temporarily darkens.
Interrupted: passage opens to the wrong era. The player enters a phantom zone where the dead stand still; escape by resolving the local Spirit debt.

### Glass (High-Risk Revelation, Self-Knowledge)

**Mirror Read** — 2 glyphs — *"Show me what I have become."*
Caster reads the Shadow's current scar-map. Sees which of their own tactics the Shadow has learned. Clarity +1 for one scene.
Interrupted: Shadow turns the mirror. Caster sees only what the Shadow *wants* them to know—a lie that feels true. Guilt +1.

**Glass Lung Descent** — 3 glyphs — *"I fall into the reflection and I do not stop falling."*
Caster enters the Spirit realm through a reflective surface. Passes through barriers; can move freely in the Spirit layer. Returns by striking the mirror again or dying. Cost: temporary weakness upon return.
Interrupted: stuck between worlds. Caster becomes translucent; neither living nor dead. Can act but cannot be seen; lasts until resolved by a faction or companion.

### Salt (Anti-Magic, Preservation, Closure)

**Decay Halt** — 1 glyph — *"The rot stops here."*
Preserve one corpse, rotting object, or Spirit breach. Stops magical decay for one day. Ledger reads "Preserved."
Interrupted: preservation reverses. The object decays *faster*. If a corpse, it animates briefly as a confused shade before collapsing to ash.

**Seal the Spirit Route** — 3 glyphs — *"The dead and living no longer speak here. The door is salt-closed."*
Permanently seal one Spirit passage or dead-route in a zone. Ghosts cannot cross; no new Tombstones can be placed here. Costs an inscribed memory (player loses one complete quest from their ledger permanently).
Interrupted: route opens wider. All nearby Spirit breaches activate at once. Summons a chorus of unfinished names that demand resolution.

## Pressure as Provenance: Whisper and Chant

**Whisper** is a cast spoken with *light touch*. The keystroke pressure is minimal—fingertip to key, quick release. The glyph inscribes *cleanly* into the Recorded ledger. There is no ambiguity: Whisper-cast actions appear in the event log with full honesty. Witnesses can attest to them. The daemon reads them back perfectly during playback. Cost: Whisper takes one extra turn to land (speech delay is the tax of truth-telling).

**Chant** is a cast spoken with *heavy pressure*. The speaker presses deep; glyphs scour the world with force. They inscribe *sharply* but incompletely—they carve into reality faster, but the ledger's ink smudges under their violence. A Chant-cast action appears in the event log as **Stolen**: partially recorded, partially erased, partially reinterpreted by the factions who later audit it. Witnesses argue about what happened. The daemon plays it back with artifacts—glitches, repeat frames, missing sound.

Cost of Chant: faster casting (save one turn per cast-word) but the ledger becomes your enemy. Every Chant opens a debt to the faction most invested in rewriting your actions. Too many Chants, and your entire event log becomes contestable—the Ledger Church can demand an official audit. Index Monks begin to decide which of your Chants "really" happened. You lose the right to be believed.

**Etiquette**: A master speaks Whisper in public (building witness consensus) and reserves Chant for desperate moments—a cut sentence that you know will scar you but that you accept anyway. Greed sounds like rapid Chant-chains, hands trembling on the keys. A skilled player learns to *hear* this in faction NPC dialogue: when an Index Monk speaks with heavy pressure, their authority over memory thins; when a Toll-Sister speaks Whisper, she is speaking covenant, not extraction.

## Rootcalling and Stonecalling: The Practice

### Rootcalling

Rootcalling is the apprenticeship of *listening into the deep*. The caster places fingertips on the earth—stone, soil, foundation—and sounds a sub-bass note into the vein of root and mineral. The tone must be *felt*, not heard; the body *is* the tuning fork. A true Rootcaller learns that every root system is already singing—the vein remembers every death buried in it, every oath sworn over it. The caster is not creating sound; they are *joining* a song that never stopped.

Cost: nosebleed. The pressure of listening deeper than breath can reach breaks the delicate vessels in the nose. A master Rootcaller bleeds constantly during a long casting. They carry cloth and accept this as the price of witness.

Effect: Rootcalling opens *feeling-knowledge*. The caster learns the shape of the underground: where passages are sealed, where roots have recently died (and why), where an oath-memory still pulses. The Bell Vein answers—not in words, but in a deep thrumming that other living things can also feel. Prisoners in deep cells know a Rootcaller has passed overhead by the tremor in the ground.

### Stonecalling

Stonecalling is the *craft* side of the practice. Where Rootcalling listens, Stonecalling *commands*. The caster strikes stone—bell-metal, foundation, masonry—with fingertip pressure and speaks the glyph aloud simultaneously. The strike creates *witness-light*: where the pressure lands, blackness fractures into crimson light, revealing Master Marks carved into the stone's underside by the first masons.

Cost: bruising along the finger-joints, eventually arthritis. A master Stonecaller's hands become gnarled and color-mottled from decades of striking. They are marked by their own power.

Effect: Stonecalling transforms *unrecorded weight* into *permanent, witnessed light*. It is the foundational act of Ironroot's metaphysics: pressure made visible is pressure made *real*, and what is real may be remembered and audited. Stonecalling creates the Recorded state. This is why it is dangerous: every Stonecall inscribes something into the world's permanent architecture. Overuse creates too much light, and too much light attracts the Shadow's attention—it learns where you strike, and it begins to strike *there* first.

**Apprenticeship**: A young Stonecaller begins with pebbles (whisper-pressure, gentle tones, private spaces). They graduate to foundation stones (chant-pressure, full commitment, public witnesses). The master test is to Stonecall the Deep Ledger itself without shattering one letter of it—one false strike, and the ledger cracks, a Tombstone forms, and the Ledger Church declares the apprentice a heretic.

## The Name-Shear: Anti-Song and Pedagogy

The **Name-Shear** is the signature sound of erasure. It is not a spell cast by the player; it is an *act committed by the city itself*. When a name, debt, memory relation, or Spirit address is being forcibly removed from the ledger, the Name-Shear sounds.

**Technically**: The Name-Shear is a descending frequency sweep—a falling siren that starts at high, clear pitch (the name as it lived) and drops into inaudible subsonic (the name as absence). The sweep takes *exactly* 1.2 seconds (144 ticks at 120Hz). As it sounds, the affected entity begins to de-exist: first memory (you forget they existed), then witnesses (NPCs forget they knew them), then ledger (the official record erases), finally architecture (the stone itself rewrites as if they never touched it).

The player's first encounter with the Name-Shear is *forced*: Morrow dies at the opening under the full sweep. No tutorial. The body learns before the mind understands. This teaches two things: (1) the sound itself is the warning, (2) hearing it does not save you—it can only prepare you.

**Why hearing it teaches it**: After 10 exposures, the player begins to recognize the sound's *shape* and can predict where it will land next. After 50 exposures, the player can *interrupt* it by striking Stone or Root before the sweep completes. At 100 exposures, the player can *prevent it from sounding*—destroying the source (often an Index Monk's ledger, a Ledger Church bell, or a faction vow-stone) before the cascade begins.

**Moral Hazard — Repetition Installs**: This is not metaphor; it is physics in Ironroot. The Name-Shear uses call-and-response pedagogy: it *calls* (the sweep sounds), and the world *responds* (the entity erases). By the fourth exposure to the same entity's erasure, the pattern *enters the player's body*. They begin to hear it in their sleep. They anticipate the sweep. At 13 exposures (a locked number), the player risks becoming *installed* with the erasure-pattern: they may accidentally speak the Name-Shear themselves, or it may begin to sound when they speak the erased entity's name, trapping them in a loop. This is how tyrants are made—through too much listening to the Shear without resistance.

**What the city makes you rehearse**:
- **Toll-Saint confessions** force daily repetition of guilt formulas—prayers that sound like the sweep's *opposite* (ascending instead of descending). Too many confessions, and the player's own ascending-prayer begins to sound like a false resolution, exhausting rather than healing.
- **Ledger audits** make NPCs repeat their allegiances in the faction's timbral signature. Repeated exposure to the Ledger Church's bell-cracking authority-sound installs *deference*—the player's own voice begins to pitch lower and slower in the Church's presence.
- **The bell-count at the Thirteen Bells Warden encounter**: the Toll-Saints make the player *hear* the exact same descending bell-interval (the grave-bell's minor third) 13 times in sequence. This is a forced installation of grief as a *musical fact*—the player's body learns to expect sadness at that interval, forever.

**Resistance**:
1. **Reject the repetition**: after recognizing the pattern, the player can *mute* by casting Ritual Silence and refusing to listen to the fourth exposure. This breaks the installation but costs Clarity (you have to deny something true about yourself).
2. **Counter-call**: Stonecall or Rootcall with *opposite contour* (ascending) at the moment the Shear sounds. This creates a duet that cancels the erasure momentarily. Cost: extreme Tarnish (you are now making light where darkness should be; you look wrong to the factions).
3. **Accept and integrate**: let the installation happen, then *use* it. By the 13th exposure, you can speak the Shear-sound yourself for a single, devastating attack. Cost: Guilt +3, and every enemy who hears it knows exactly what you are now capable of.

## Call-and-Response: The Base Protocol

Communication in Ironroot is *never* monologue. Every spell, every faction address, every Spirit contact operates through **call-and-response**.

**Stonecalling** is the call: the player strikes stone and speaks a glyph aloud. This creates pressure—a demand for the world to answer.

**The Bell Vein answers**: The response lands exactly on the next strong beat of the 10-second Metronome breath. The Bell Vein (the deepest layer of the root system, which remembers all oaths) resonates at the same pitch as the call, or at the harmonic one-third or one-fifth. This resonance is *witnessed*: it creates the crimson light, reveals the Master Marks, inscribes the action into the Recorded ledger.

**Descending contour = grief (physics, not convention)**: The grave-bell's minor third naturally triggers a sensory response of *resignation* and *loss*. This is in the cochlea, not the culture. A call that descends feels like a wound opening. A response that descends feels like acceptance of that wound. This is why the Toll-Saints use the descending interval in their chants: they are making the listener *feel* the acceptability of debt.

**Faction sound as address**:
- **Ledger Church**: cathedral bell cracking into static. Response is silence (acceptance of authority).
- **Toll-Saints**: chain-rattle pulse cut short. Response is the body's flinch (acceptance of toll-payment).
- **Index Monks**: page-flip swarm into silence. Response is confusion (memory being rewritten).
- **Anvil Covenant**: hammer-ring that remembers. Response is impact-tremor (the oath-weapon accepting blood-memory).
- **Widow Courts**: drowned vow, water pressure. Response is submersion (drowning in inheritance law).
- **Hollow Astronomers**: vacuum pulse with missing echo. Response is vertigo (the world stepping sideways into correspondence).
- **Root-Pruners**: blade-through-bone transient. Response is stillness (the body refusing to be noticed).
- **Free Graves**: wind, or no sound. Response is: nothing. No answer. This is refusal itself—the sound of debtlessness.

A player who learns to *recognize* each faction's timbral address can speak to them without words. The player simply calls in the faction's own timbre, and the faction *must* respond—it is bound by the call-and-response covenant. This is how the player gains leverage: by forcing the factions to answer for their own sounds.

## Interlocking Incompleteness: The Duet Mechanic

One core spell-pattern uses **interlocking incompleteness**: two cast-words that are each *individually invalid* but together form a complete, resonant phrase.

**Example: Forge-Partner Rhythm** (used in combat with an Anvil Covenant smith or companion)

**Player cast:** "I strike." (1 glyph)
**Partner cast:** "The hammer remembers." (2 glyphs)
**Combined meaning:** "I strike, and the hammer remembers—the blow becomes legendary and inscribed."

If the player speaks "I strike" and the partner *does not answer* with "The hammer remembers," the player's cast fails; they take 1 pressure damage from the false resonance.

If the partner speaks "The hammer remembers" without the player's prior call, it is meaningless—the hammer has nothing to remember yet. The partner wastes their glyphs and takes Tarnish +1 for the orphaned cast.

**Only together** do they form a complete duet. The rhythm must be: call (player) → anticipation (1-turn pause, both holding breath) → response (partner) → impact (the blow lands as Recorded, gains +2 pressure, costs no additional vigor).

This duet mechanic teaches the player that *casting is not individual heroism*—it is relationship, covenant, trust. A player who learns to trust a companion can execute spells neither could speak alone. This is the mechanical shadow of the game's deep theme: grief cannot be resolved alone; it requires witness, call-and-response, the body of another person.

**Cost of failure**: If the partner flinches or hesitates, the call-and-response breaks. The resulting cascade creates an **improper resonance**—the world notes the failure as a Recorded breaking of covenant. The Widow Courts take note; the Anvil Covenant's trust decays.

## Learning Other Factions' Grammars: Bard-Adjacent Play

A player can learn to speak the *grammars* of other factions by studying their sound signatures and the NPCs who embody them.

**Grammar structure**: phrase + carrier + authority + validator

- **Phrase**: the exact words or tonal sequence (e.g., "I accept the toll" or the bell-crack-static pattern).
- **Carrier**: the medium through which the phrase moves (voice, hammer-strike, page-turning, chain-rattle).
- **Authority**: who is licensed to speak this phrase (Toll-Saints speak tolls; Ledger Church speaks law; Free Graves speak wind).
- **Validator**: what makes the phrase *land*—what proves it has been Recorded (witnesses, ledger-scribe, the responsive bell).

**Example: Speaking Toll-Saint Authority**

If the player studies Toll-Sister Vey at the Toll Gate long enough (watching her collect tolls, hearing her repeat the chain-rattle pulse, witnessing her authority), the player can eventually *speak Toll-Saint*:

- **Phrase**: "The toll is honored. Pass, or kneel."
- **Carrier**: the player must strike a chain or bell-piece they carry (or strike the Toll Gate itself) in the same rhythm Vey uses (CHINK-CHINK-pause-CHINK).
- **Authority**: the player has no inherent authority—they are *pretending* to be a Toll-Sister. This is dangerous. If a real Toll-Saint witnesses the mimicry, they may allow it (treating the player as an honorary collector) or *reject* it (calling the player a false prophet; Tarnish +2, and Toll-Saints become hostile).
- **Validator**: a witness must accept the toll as if it were real. If they bow or step back, the grammar has landed. If they laugh or attack, the mimicry has failed.

This creates a playstyle where the player becomes a *speaker-between-factions*, a voice that can translate and negotiate. It is high-risk: one faction may offer the player shelter by teaching them their grammar, but a rival faction will punish the player for *sounding like the enemy*. The player's own voice becomes marked—factions will hear the accents of the ones they studied.

**Master speakers can hold multiple faction grammars in one scene**, switching timbre mid-conversation. This is the rarest and most dangerous skill. A player who fluently speaks Toll-Saint, then Ledger Church, then Free Graves—all in one negotiation—reveals themselves as either a diplomatic genius or a chaos agent. The world reacts accordingly.

## Summary: The Cost Is Always Spoken

Every cast-word is a *sentence*. Its length in glyphs is its cost in blood, ash, or memory. Pressure is provenance: light Whisper-touch inscribes truth; heavy Chant-pressure inscribes stolen possibility.

The world answers in timbre. Call-and-response is the only law. Learn to *hear* the city's voice, and you can become one of its speakers. The Name-Shear teaches erasure through repetition; repetition installs; installation costs resistance.

The smallest duet—player and partner, or player and daemon—is the only way to speak what cannot be spoken alone. Grief, once given back its name and its witness, becomes bearable. Ironroot rewards those who choose to *be heard* over those who choose to *be unheard*.

The Bell Vein remembers every strike.
