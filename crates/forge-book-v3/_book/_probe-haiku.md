## V. Research Haiku

*written while sitting with the throughline: descent, withholding, and the gap between event and sentence — plus three on frame-narrative technique (Scheherazade's stalling frame, the Panchatantra's nested tiger, the deliberately unclosed outer frame)*

1.
> seven gates, each one
> takes a garment — by the floor
> she is only voice

2.
> the punchline arrives
> one syllable before dark —
> rakugo, lights out

3.
> Gunnar was killed. Njal
> said nothing. the saga moves
> on without a wound

4.
> copied by hand, twice
> wrong — so I say it three ways;
> one survives the fall

5.
> knock, knock, knock: not the
> miner. the story already
> knows what the cave means

6.
> a story to stall
> the axe — a thousand and one
> frames, none of them close

7.
> the tiger asks the
> jackal for a story — inside
> it, another tiger

8.
> outer frame left open
> on purpose — the moral walks
> out and finds the reader

---

*Compiled as a working reference — not exhaustive, deliberately weighted toward structural technique over cultural packaging.*
