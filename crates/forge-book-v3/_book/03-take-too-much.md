## Two ways to hear it

The old people split a story in two before they tell it. One kind is sacred. It is how the world was shaped, back when the animals and the people still shared speech. The other kind is the record. It happened last winter, or a hundred winters back, and you can walk to the spot. This land is both. Take the cold spirit as a story if you want. The record is dated and it is on the map. You do not have to believe the one to answer for the other. Both are true.

## The cold comes

There is a spirit that eats. It is greed with a mouth. It comes in the deep cold, in the starving months, and it hollows a person out and fills them with ice. The Cree call it wîhtiko. The law under it is short. Take too much and the cold comes. In 1879, near Fort Saskatchewan, a Cree trapper named Swift Runner took too much. He ate his own family through the winter and said the cold had gotten in. They hanged him in the spring. The story is old. The winter it names is not.

## The water remembers

Water is not a thing here. It is a living body, and it keeps a memory. It holds what was said on its shore and what was done to it. So listen to what the water has been made to hold. In 1929 they dammed the Bow and drowned Stoney Nakoda land. They named the reservoir Ghost Lake. In 2005 a train spilled seven hundred thousand litres of oil into Wabamun, the mirror lake, and the fish went with it. Downstream from the oil sands, the people of Fort Chipewyan started counting cancers. The water remembers all of it. See it from the road.

## The little people at the cliff

Not every spirit is the cold. In the rapids and the cliff faces live the small ones, the memekwesiwak. They move your markers. They throw sound from the wrong direction. They are not cruel. They test a person. They punish the greedy hand and they help the humble one. Walk their ground taking only what you need and you pass. Walk it grabbing and the daylight turns you around. Greed gets you lost.

## The prospector

The cold learned to wear a company name. It stopped starving and started digging. The old word for the sin is the depth of a plough, cutting deeper than the land gave leave. Read the ledger. Frank, 1903: the mountain came down in the dark and buried ninety people in their beds. Hillcrest, 1914: the gas lit and killed a hundred and eighty-nine men underground, the worst in the country. Port Radium hauled its ore down through here to arm a bomb, and the Dene men who carried it came home to a village of widows. Same spirit. New teeth. Same cold.

## The ghost moon

There are thirteen moons in the year and the last one has no face. It is present by being named absent. The residential schools are not drawn on this land and they are on this land. The drowned village is under the reservoir and it is still the village. You walk a country with no cities and no people rendered, only the geomapped ground and the weight of what happened at each stone. One character stands on the screen. It is the land. The only law that heals is to give back more than you take. That is the thirteenth moon. Name it the ghost. It is there.

## Why I tell it this way

I am Cree, from Treaty 6. My father was never taught this. My grandfather had to run from it. So I am not going to dress it up for you. I am going to hand you the receipt. You do not need to believe in a spirit to read a dead lake, or a village of widows, or a tailings pond you can see from orbit. The old people named the disease three hundred years before the oil proved them right. Take too much and the cold comes. That is not superstition. That is the invoice. Read it or don't.
