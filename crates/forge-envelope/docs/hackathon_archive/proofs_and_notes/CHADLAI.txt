> Here's the breakdown, hardest difference first.

  1. rekordbox knows the song's structure. Mixxx knows where the silence is.

  rekordbox runs phrase analysis (their KORETECH analyzer) and labels every section of the track: Intro / Up / Down / Chorus / Bridge / Verse / Outro. Automix then picks mix-in
and mix-out points at
  phrase boundaries, and additionally uses beat position, BPM and key.

  Mixxx has four cue markers  intro_start, intro_end, outro_start, outro_end  and they're set primarily by silence detection. That tells it where audio begins and ends, not where the 32-
bar chorus
  resolves. AutoDJProcessor::calculateTransition() compares outro length vs. intro length and takes the shorter as the crossfade window.

  This is the one that makes it sound wrong. A transition that starts 11 bars into a phrase reads as "bad DJing" to a listener even when the fade curve itself is perfect. Mixxx is landing
  transitions on a silence-derived timestamp; rekordbox is landing them on a downbeat at a musical boundary.

  2. Auto DJ doesn't beatmatch at all

  Auto DJ performs no sync of its own. You get beatmatching only if you manually latch Sync Lock on both decks so the sync button "sticks"  and even then there's no gradual tempo ramp,
and
  non-constant beatgrids break it (issue #9795 (https://github.com/mixxxdj/mixxx/issues/9795)). Beatmatching in Auto DJ is still an open feature request (#14067
  (https://github.com/mixxxdj/mixxx/issues/14067), #6351 (https://github.com/mixxxdj/mixxx/issues/6351)).

  Smart Fader on the FLX4 syncs the incoming track on-beat, then slides both tracks' BPM across the transition so you land on the new track's native tempo. That's what lets it bridge a
12498 BPM
  genre jump without either track sounding stretched at the destination.

  3. Mixxx's crossfade is a linear volume ramp on two full-spectrum tracks

  No EQ automation. Two tracks with full bass overlapping for 830 seconds = kick-drum phase mush and loudness pumping. Bass-swapping is the single most audible thing a human DJ does with
their
  hands, and Auto DJ doesn't do it.

  Smart Fader automates EQ LOW ducking across the fader travel and drops an echo tail on the outgoing track as it decays. Even if it did nothing else, killing the bass clash alone would
account for
  most of the perceived quality gap.

  The Mixxx manual is blunt about this: "Auto DJ does not take into account the volume of each track, nor the frequency content, nor the rhythms, so it's not intended to be a replacement for
a human
  DJ." (ReplayGain does handle static per-track level, but nothing dynamic during the blend.)

  4. Track selection

  Automix factors key into how it mixes. Auto DJ plays queue order, or pulls random tracks from a source playlist  zero harmonic logic, zero energy-curve logic.

  5. Scope is the real asymmetry

  rekordbox's automix is tuned for a narrow target: 4/4 club music, stable tempo, conventional 8/16/32-bar phrasing. Inside that box you can be very good. Mixxx's Auto DJ is genre-agnostic and
  explicitly not trying to be a DJ. Pioneer solved a smaller problem well; Mixxx solved a larger problem shallowly.

  ---

  What you can actually do about it in Mixxx

  Roughly in order of payoff:

  1. Hand-place the intro/outro cues on phrase boundaries and use Full Intro + Outro or Fade At Outro Start. This is the big one  it retrofits structural awareness manually, and it's
the only fix
  that addresses cause #1.
  2. Latch Sync Lock on both decks + keylock before starting Auto DJ. Fixes #2 partially (no ramp, but no clash).
  3. midiAutoDJ (https://github.com/budul100/midiAutoDJ)  hybrid engine that decides beatmatch-vs-fade based on tempo delta, plus automatic phrase matching. rdircio/autodj
  (https://github.com/rdircio/autodj)  harmonic mixing with key transposition during the blend, restored after. These exist because the gap is real, and they prove it's closable at the
  controller-script layer without touching Mixxx's C++.

  The genuinely hard part to replicate is phrase detection  that's a trained structural-segmentation model, not a heuristic. Everything else in the list (bass swap, tempo ramp, downbeat
alignment)
  is achievable with the beatgrid Mixxx already computes.
