The artifact

A lexer converts a character stream into meaning-bearing tokens. Whitespace tokenization assumes word ≈ semantic unit. In a polysynthetic language that is simply false — niwâpamâw is one whitespace token carrying actor, verb, goal, direction and order. Every pipeline downstream of a whitespace tokenizer is parsing garbage before it starts.

An FST morphological analyzer is a lexer in the strict compiler sense: finite-state, ahead of the parser, emitting typed tokens. One component, four functions:

FunctionWhat the lexer provides
Anti-hallucinationDefines the legal emission space
5D latticeEach analysis is a coordinate
Clinical biomarker fixTTR, pronoun/noun ratio, pause boundaries computed on morphemes, not whitespace
MultimodalShared alignment across SRO, syllabics, audio

The Twist — 40% of the score

Vocal and linguistic biomarkers for cognitive decline are calibrated on English morphology. A Cree-speaking elder screened with that instrument is being measured with the wrong ruler. The lexer closes that gap.

Architecture

Blocking correction

Vertex/Gemini does not support GBNF or per-token logit masking. It supports controlled generation via responseSchema — structured JSON — only. Any plan built on "logit-masked Gemini emission" is not implementable on the required platform. This invalidated the original design.

Corrected pipeline, buildable on Vertex today:

Gemini 3.5 (Vertex, responseSchema) emits a COORDINATE, never Cree characters {"class":"VTA","actor":"1SG","goal":"3AN","order":"independent"} │ Clingo / ASP integrity constraints reject illegal coordinates │ (obviation; direction on the 2>1>3>3′ hierarchy) FST (generation mode) the ONLY thing that emits Cree text │ niwâpamâw │ .glb lattice witness surface — legal cells lit, holes dark

Hallucinated Cree is impossible not because logits are masked, but because Gemini is never in the Cree character-generation path at all. Stronger than masking, and it ships.

The division of labour is compiler-theoretic: lexer (FST, regular) + constraint solver (ASP, beyond-regular). Obviation and direction are long-distance dependencies within a single token — past what regular power handles. That is why Clingo earns its place rather than decorating the stack.

.glb is transport, not constraint. Constraints are discharged by the solver before anything reaches the container.

Pitch boundaries

Cite as prior art: two-level finite-state morphology, Giellatekno/ALTLab FSTs, twenty years across dozens of languages. Claiming the FST as novel fails instantly against a domain-literate judge.

Claim as novel: (1) FST-derived constraint over polysynthetic morphology in a low-resource setting — narrow it, since grammar-constrained decoding is itself published work; (2) morpheme-level acoustic and linguistic biomarker extraction — strongest claim, unverified; (3) the spatial-linguistic coordinate mapping — weak, demote to a design note.

The isomorphism table is two-of-five

  • Real — Lexer. Deterministic segmentation of a continuous signal into typed discrete units. letter_morphometrics does this on glyphs; the FST does it on morphemes. Same operation, different signal.
  • Real — Constraint Solver. Literally the same Clingo on two domains, not an analogy.
  • Forced — Raw Input. "Both pipelines have input" does no work.
  • Forced — AST / Lattice. Equivocates a discrete categorical tuple with continuous vertex algebra. Different kinds of object.
  • Forced — Decoder Mask. A render pipeline draws; it does not constrain. Position in a pipeline is not structural identity.

Say shared front-end discipline, not "isomorphism." Two solid correspondences carry a pitch; a claimed bijection that is two-of-five invites someone to check the other three.

Overclaim risk

Framing this as "fundamental AI infrastructure" makes judges evaluate it as infrastructure — benchmarks, evals, generalization. A ten-day demo cannot answer that. Say the lexer solves a real structural problem here and the approach generalizes. Same trap as "proven 100%," in a better suit.
