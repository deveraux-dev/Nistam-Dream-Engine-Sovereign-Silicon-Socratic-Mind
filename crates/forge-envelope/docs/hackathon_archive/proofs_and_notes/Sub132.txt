  ### 1. Inspiration: From 23 Years on Industrial Steel to Sovereign Code

  I dropped out of school in Grade 9 because standard institutional classrooms were not built for
  a brain like mine. For the next 23 years, I put material on steel across Edmonton, Suncor, Fort
  McMurray, and the arches of the Walterdale Bridge—working as a commercial painter, sandblaster,
  lead abatement specialist, and earning my NACE Level 2 Coating Inspector certification.

  In industrial coatings, surfaces lie to you constantly. A freshly coated structural beam can
  look immaculate to the naked eye while catastrophic galvanic corrosion festers underneath
  because a contractor skipped the surface profile prep or coated below the dew point. On job
  sites, disputes bleed billions of dollars: general contractors hold back payments based on
  subjective visual opinions, while independent tradespeople, small subcontractors, and First
  Nations communities on Treaty land have zero cryptographic tools to defend their physical
  workmanship.

  When I was diagnosed with AuDHD at age 39, everything clicked. My brain cannot do long division
  on paper because working memory drops intermediate states, exactly like a CPU floating-point
  unit drifts over long iteration chains. In painting, if you skip the scraper, the paint peels;
  in computer systems, if you tolerate floating-point drift, heap fragmentation, and unverified
  data retention, the system collapses under stress.

  a compiler."
